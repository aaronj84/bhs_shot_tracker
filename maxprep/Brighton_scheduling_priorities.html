Priorities, ranked by influence on final ranking

1Beat good teams. Single biggest lever.

A win over a top-15 opponent moves us more than anything else. The rating asks how strong we must be to have beaten who we beat — only strong opponents force a high answer.

2Do not lose to weak teams. Biggest downside.

An unexpected loss gets more expensive the weaker the opponent. A 1–0 loss to a bottom-half team costs far more than a 1–0 loss to a top-5 team. Take every game seriously.

3Schedule up in non-region games.

Str is 3.8 and can only climb out of region. Target roughly the top 20 in the state, any classification. A competitive loss to a genuinely strong team barely hurts; a win is the best result available. Two or three of these, not a whole schedule of them.

4Classification is irrelevant.

The rating never uses class. Crimson Cliffs (4A) is #1 in the state and Green Canyon (4A) is #3. A game against the top of 4A is a better rating asset than a game against the bottom of 6A. Schedule by the rating list.

5Do not run up the score past five goals.

Soccer is capped at five. A 9–0 and a 5–0 are the same number. Everything past the fifth goal is injury risk and goodwill we need next year.

6Report scores fast and correctly — especially out of state.

If we play out of state, it is our job to get those scores entered. Missing opponent games make our Str wrong. Enter non-varsity as “Non-Varsity Opponent.”

7Prefer opponents who will be good later, not just now.

About half of what moves our rating from here is what past opponents do the rest of the season. A September win over a team that becomes a November power gets re-credited at full value.

Region is locked — and that is why non-region has to carry the load

We play each of Bountiful, Olympus, Viewmont, Woods Cross, Alta, and Skyline twice. Mixed bag:

Helping Str

Olympus #7 (16.27) and Bountiful #10 (13.98). Four real games.

Dragging Str

Alta (−2.96), Woods Cross (3.56), Viewmont (8.10), Skyline (4.85). Unavoidable.

Half the slate is fixed at mediocre average strength. The other half is the only lever.

One-sentence version

Play and beat the strongest teams we can find out of region, never lose to a weak one, ignore what class anyone is in, and stop caring about the scoreline once we are up five.

If asked: swap a #75 win for a close #5 loss?

Direction is clear. That trade almost certainly helps, or costs nearly nothing: drop a cheap win (Logan, rating about −5.25) and add a top-5 opponent (Lone Peak, 17.87). MaxPreps says a close loss to a much higher-rated team can raise your rating. Magnitude — how many seed spots — is not a number anyone should invent until the full season game list is loaded. The machinery exists; the inputs are not in yet.

Directions above are grounded in MaxPreps/UHSAA published rules and the 13 Sep 2026 state rankings. Exact seed value of any single game is not calibrated. Treat this as an order of operations, not a forecast. MaxPreps last updated this list 10 Sep 2026.
